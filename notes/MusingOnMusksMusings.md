# Musing on Musk’s musings

A question posed against the surplus-value and accumulation increments, and an answer in the same materialist frame. Not a new class diagram. The types named below are those already defined in [surplus-value.md](surplus-value.md) and [accumulation.md](accumulation.md).

---

## The question

For Marx, the possibility of replacing all human labour with machines in the means of production was not a conceivable possibility at the time. Yet it is being talked about now — AI and robotics replacing all human labour in the production processes, including extraction, processing and transportation of raw materials.

What would be a social system that could accommodate such a change?

---

## The answer

The social system that can accommodate that change is **production for use under common ownership** — Marx’s communism, the SPGB’s socialism. Capitalism cannot complete it and remain capitalism.

Marx did not treat total replacement of living labour as a near-term industrial fact. He did treat the *direction*: machinery as objectified labour confronting living labour, a rising share of constant capital (`c`) relative to variable capital (`v`), and — in the *Grundrisse* “Fragment on Machines” — the point at which labour-time stops being a rational measure of wealth. The hypothetical is that fragment taken to its limit.

### Why the existing types break

In the model already built:

- `LivingLabour` is the only source of *new* value. Machines are `MeansOfProduction` / `ConstantCapital`: they transfer past labour; they do not expand `v` into `s`.
- `SurplusValue` is unpaid *labour*. No living labour in extraction, processing, or transport means no `s`.
- `LabourPowerAsCommodity` is the hinge of the whole capitalist form. If that commodity is no longer bought because it is no longer needed, `WageLabourer` and `Capital` lose their relation.
- `CapitalistCompetition` still compels each capital to automate (cheapen, displace labour). That compulsion, pushed far enough, abolishes the condition the compulsion itself lives on: wage-labour as the source of surplus value.

So a fully automated *capitalist* production process is a contradiction in terms, not a new equilibrium. You can have automated *use-value* production. You cannot have automated *valorization*. `LabourProcess` can in principle be conducted by instruments humans have built; `ValorizationProcess` cannot, because it *is* the counting of living labour-time beyond the equivalent of `v`.

### What remains, and what falls away

| Stays (transhistorical) | Falls away (historically specific) |
| --- | --- |
| `Nature`, metabolism, energy, materials | `Value`, `AbstractLabour`, SNLT |
| `UseValue` as the content of wealth | `Commodity` as the social form of wealth |
| Instruments, infrastructure, knowledge | `Capital`, `SurplusValue`, `Wage` |
| Need, and the need to *decide* what is produced | `LabourPowerAsCommodity`, class monopoly of the means of life |

The labour process does not vanish as a *material* process. Nature still has to be transformed. What vanishes is labour as the social substance of wealth and the wage as the ticket to consume.

### The social form that fits

A society that can live on that technical basis has to do three things the value-form did automatically (and blindly):

1. **Own the automated means of life in common** — otherwise a class that monopolizes the robots still monopolizes access to use-values, and the rest of the population has neither wages nor a claim on the product. That is not “post-scarcity capitalism”; it is class monopoly without even the wage relation.
2. **Produce for use, not for sale.** If products are no longer values, they cannot be allocated by price. Allocation has to be a conscious social decision: what to extract, process, move, and make, given material constraints (energy, materials, ecology) rather than socially necessary labour-time.
3. **Detach consumption from the sale of labour-power.** “From each according to ability, to each according to need” is not a moral slogan here; it is the only distribution rule left once there is no wage and no value. Ability, in the limit described, is mostly the capacity to participate in deciding and maintaining the automated system, not to expend labour-time in the old sense.

That is why SPGB socialism (common ownership, democratic control, production solely for use, no money, no wages, no state as a class machine) is the form that *fits* the technical change, rather than a policy that tries to patch capitalism after the wage has lost its function.

### What does not accommodate it

- **Capitalism plus UBI / robot tax.** Those still presuppose money, prices, and a surplus to redistribute. If living labour has ceased to create value, there is no coherent surplus-value fund to tax; you are left printing claims on use-values while the class that owns the machines still decides what exists. It is a political attempt to keep the surface of the wage-form after its material basis has gone.
- **State-owned robots, still selling commodities.** Changing who personifies capital (the same point as in [accumulation.md §5](accumulation.md#5-why-this-rules-out-political-choice)) does not restore valorization if there is no living labour, and does not abolish class monopoly if access still runs through the state-as-owner.
- **“The AIs own themselves.”** Ownership is a social relation among people about things. Machines do not become a new class; people still either share the means of life or are excluded from them.

### A materialist limit on the thought-experiment

“All human labour” in extraction, processing, *and* transport is stronger than any present technique. Design, energy capture, rare-earths, repair, ecological side-effects, and care of living people remain material processes. The theoretical point does not require the last human to clock off. It requires only that labour-time cease to be the necessary social measure of wealth. At that point the historically specific types (`Value`, `Capital`, `Wage`) become inapplicable, and the transhistorical ones (`Nature`, `UseValue`, a labour process now mostly objectified in instruments) have to be governed by a different social form.

If this were modelled later, it would be a distinct increment: not more determinations *inside* capitalism, but the point at which those determinations no longer apply — a contrast diagram, like `SimpleCommodityProduction` already is for surplus value. It would not sit on the same abstract-to-concrete spine as accumulation.
